> Good point. If we want accessibility and user-uploaded content, here are some alternatives:

### **1. Citizen Science Biofilm Tracking**

- Users upload photos of biofilms from everyday environments (e.g., ponds, damp walls, kitchen drains, etc.).
- Other users classify colors, textures, and patterns (e.g., slimy, patchy, fibrous).
- The minigame assigns planetary equivalents: different biofilm types would correspond to bacterial colonies in the terrarium.

**Real-world impact:**

- Helps track microbial colonization in urban/rural environments.
- Supports research on antibiotic resistance, biofilm formation, and decomposition.

---

### **2. Fermentation & Food Spoilage Explorer**

- Users upload images of food decay (moldy bread, fermenting fruits, etc.).
- Others classify by mold/bacteria type, spread pattern, and color.
- In-game, these contribute to microbial diversity and decomposition cycles.

**Real-world impact:**

- Creates a database of common household bacteria and fungi.
- Helps identify beneficial vs. harmful strains for food safety and preservation research.

---

### **3. Water Sample Biome Mapper**

- Users submit images of pond water, puddles, or even aquarium surfaces.
- Other users classify based on turbidity, color, visible particles, or patterns on the surface.
- Certain classifications correlate to microbial ecosystems that could exist in the terrarium.

**Real-world impact:**

- Could help track pollution levels and microbial water quality.
- Supports research on how bacteria and protozoa thrive in different water conditions.

---

Would any of these work for your vision, or do you want something even simpler?
