### **Frozen Biome**

**Temperature Range:** -180°C to -50°C  
**Key Data Contributions:**

- **Cloudspotting on Mars:** Water-ice clouds suggest potential subsurface ice deposits or cold high-altitude regions.
- **Planet Four:** Presence of CO₂ ice fans/blotches, along with spider formations, indicates extreme cold and sublimation cycles.
- **AI 4 Mars:** Terrain features like rocky or icy plains support frozen landscapes.
- **Jovian Vortex Hunters:** Ammonia or ammonium hydrosulfide clouds imply frigid atmospheres on gas giants.  
    **Formation:** Mining for water or CO₂ ice in high latitudes or shadowed regions.

### **Lush Biome**

**Temperature Range:** 15°C to 35°C  
**Key Data Contributions:**

- **Cloudspotting on Mars:** Dense cloud coverage and precipitation signals atmospheric water vapor.
- **Planet Hunters:** Planets in the habitable zone of their parent star support conditions for forests and greenery.
- **AI 4 Mars:** Soil suitable for vegetation can be inferred from terrain classifications.  
    **Formation:** Requires weather patterns providing rainfall and a suitable atmosphere composition (oxygen and nitrogen).

### **Beach Biome (Includes Lakes)**

**Temperature Range:** 0°C to 40°C  
**Key Data Contributions:**

- **Planet Four:** Evidence of seasonal melting and runoff channels near polar regions.
- **Planet Hunters:** Classification of planets with a water signature from light curves or atmospheric data.
- **AI 4 Mars:** Smooth terrains that suggest previous liquid activity or shorelines.  
    **Formation:** Liquid solvents (water, methane) create rivers, lakes, or seas. Surface height classifications can determine the boundaries of water bodies.

### **Mountain/Hills Biome**

**Temperature Range:** -50°C to 15°C  
**Key Data Contributions:**

- **Cloudspotting on Mars:** Gravity wave clouds near elevated terrains indicate wind interaction with mountains.
- **AI 4 Mars:** Rugged and uneven terrain classifications suggest elevated regions.  
    **Formation:** Terrain height is key; interaction with winds creates dynamic weather patterns.

### **Desert/Arid Biome**

**Temperature Range:** 10°C to 50°C  
**Key Data Contributions:**

- **Planet Four:** Erosion patterns from sublimation suggest arid regions.
- **AI 4 Mars:** Classification of sandy or rocky terrains supports desert biomes.
- **Jovian Vortex Hunters:** Dust storms and arid atmospheres on terrestrial planets mimic Martian conditions.

### **Atmosphere/Clouds Biome**

**Temperature Range:** -180°C to 50°C (depends on altitude and composition)  
**Key Data Contributions:**

- **Jovian Vortex Hunters:** Cyclonic/anti-cyclonic features simulate layered atmospheric dynamics.
- **Cloudspotting on Mars:** High-altitude CO₂ or water-ice clouds provide an in-cloud environment.

### **Ocean Biome** (Liquid World)

**Temperature Range:** -100°C to 30°C (varies by solvent, e.g., methane or water)  
**Key Data Contributions:**

- **Planet Hunters:** Planets with signs of subsurface oceans or dense liquid atmospheres.
- **Cloudspotting on Mars:** Water vapor and precipitation patterns point to liquid solvents.  
    **Formation:** Requires a combination of atmospheric pressures and temperatures conducive to liquid formation.

### **Proposed Mechanisms for Biome Integration**

1. **Temperature-Driven Zoning:** Planets with multiple biomes distribute them based on temperature gradients and terrain height.
2. **Composition Layers:** Cloud compositions (e.g., water, methane, ammonia) define upper layers of gaseous planets, while surface features (spiders, fans) determine terrestrial zones.
3. **Dynamic Biome Events:** Seasonal data from anomalies like fans or sublimation cycles create transient biomes (e.g., icy deserts converting to lakes).
