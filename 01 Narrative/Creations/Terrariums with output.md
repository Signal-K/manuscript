Here’s how **anomalies from each project** inform biome generation:

#### **Jovian Vortex Hunters**

- **Vortices:** Generate atmospheric biomes with swirling, stormy skies. Resource abundance is tied to storm size, colour, and dynamics:
    
    - **Red/Brown Vortices (Brown Barges):** High-energy storms producing rare exotic gases like metallic hydrogen or ammonia.
    - **Turbulent Regions:** Host storm-adapted creatures (e.g., gas-bag animals or lightning-resistant lifeforms) and support weather-driven mining operations.
    - **Anticyclonic Features (Negative Vorticity):** Create stable atmospheric zones with floating islands rich in resources (e.g., ammonia crystals).
    - **Cyclonic Features:** Spawn dynamic biomes with fast-evolving ecosystems, stormy skies, and resources tied to chaotic weather.
- **Gameplay Tie-In:** Users could terraform storms to stabilize them for mining or allow dynamic weather to persist, encouraging unique lifeforms to evolve.
    

#### **Planet Four**

- **Fans:** Indicate windy regions, suggesting resource deposits tied to **wind-driven transport** (e.g., CO₂ layers or mineral deposits eroded by wind). They generate **desert or arid biomes** with vast dunes, but also potential subterranean water pockets.
    
- **Blotches:** Suggest calmer, wind-free zones where **surface features dominate**, producing **stable biomes** such as rocky deserts with potential for hardy lifeforms.
    
- **Spiders (Araneiforms):** Generate biome features with unique subsurface channels, potentially indicating underground networks for mining or water access.
    
- **Gameplay Tie-In:** The sublimation process (CO₂ ice turning to gas) drives biome changes, where users manage seasonal resource availability (e.g., gather ice deposits in winter, water in spring).
    

#### **AI 4 Mars**

- **Terrain Types:**
    
    - Rocky regions = spawn **mountainous or canyon-like biomes**, rich in mineral resources.
    - Sandy regions = produce **desert ecosystems**, with creatures adapted to arid conditions.
    - Cratered or layered terrain = creates **diverse, uneven terrains**, where rivers and lakes might emerge from subsurface melting.
- **Gameplay Tie-In:** Users explore classified terrain to determine where it’s safe to expand terrariums. Rovers or tools help map resources tied to terrain types, like sand for silica-based resources or craters for potential water ice.
    

#### **Cloudspotting on Mars**

- **Distinct Cloud Shapes:** Indicate active weather systems (precipitation, storms), driving **dynamic biomes** like lakes or frozen regions.
- **Cloudy Regions:** Suggest subsurface ice deposits or seasonal resource availability. Biomes here might fluctuate seasonally, with lifeforms adapted to changing climates.
- **No Clouds:** Generate static, arid terrains, suitable for **dry desert biomes** or hardy organisms like lichens.

### **Biome-Determining Factors**

Here’s a simplified flow tying all four projects into terrarium generation:

|**Citizen Science Data**|**Biome Output**|
|---|---|
|**Jovian Vortex (Storm Type)**|Atmosphere (stormy sky islands), frozen zones, or hybrid lush biomes (storm-fed ecosystems).|
|**Planet Four (Fans/Blotches)**|Arid/desert biomes (wind-driven fans), stable rocky deserts (blotches), or underground networks (spiders).|
|**AI 4 Mars (Terrain Type)**|Mountainous or canyon biomes, deserts, or crater-fed lakes.|
|**Cloudspotting (Cloud Shape)**|Dynamic water-based biomes (lakes/oceans) or frozen zones.|
|**Planet Hunters (Exoplanets)**|Planet temperature or distance from star modifies biome types: e.g., ocean biomes for warm worlds.|
